When I volunteered to help with [the Billys](https://www.thebillys.org/) winter gathering, the coordinators asked if I'd be willing to organize a collaborative art project on the theme: Myth Weaving.
The central ideas originated from the "Four Directions of a Queer Person" from a workshop called [The four directions of a queer man](https://schooloflostborders.org/programs_courses/the-four-directions-of-a-queer-man-forgiveness-apology-reconciliation-4/) offered by the School of Lost Borders.

Drawing inspiration from African headdresses, Native American totems, the art of [Verasphere](https://verasphere.com/), and [Nick Cave](https://art21.org/artist/nick-cave/), I conceived of 4 modular forms about 2.5 feet high. I envisioned simple tube shapes that were also stackable, but with eye holes so they could be worn as headdresses. After discussions with other Billys, I settled on woven baskets for the base form.

I asked the gathering attendees to bring fabric items, baubles, adornments, decorations, or up-cycled items to add the sculptures. 
Over the course of the gathering, many Billys participated. Each day we decorated one basket according to the day’s theme: we added fabric, baubles, dried hydrangeas, and all sorts of things (including a cock ring) to create unique designs.

In the end, we displayed the headdresses as a totem in the Heart Lodge at Saratoga Springs. As part of the closing ritual, we deconstructed the headdresses and folks wore them in the ceremony.

Even though this was my concept, the execution was 100% collaborative.  For fear of forgetting someone, I am hesitant to name the folks individually who contributed. I am gateful to everyone who showed up and contributed to this beautiful project.
